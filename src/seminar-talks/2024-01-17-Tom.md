---
title: 'Dynamicalization and categorification'
speaker: 'Tom Leinster'
---
Dynamicalization is the process of taking a concept that applies to static objects (such as spaces) and extending it to objects that evolve through time (such as spaces equipped with an action by \\(\mathbb{Z}\\) or \\(\mathbb{R}\\)). The term was introduced, I believe, by Masaki Tsukamoto.

Dynamicalization is similar in flavour to categorification, and should be amenable to a systematic categorical approach. It could even have the same massive scope as categorification &ndash; even though it has received only a tiny fraction of the attention. In fact, there is a sense in which dynamicalization appears to be dual to categorification. I will explain what I understand so far, give some examples, and try to convey what I think is the promise of this big idea. There will be many more questions than answers.
