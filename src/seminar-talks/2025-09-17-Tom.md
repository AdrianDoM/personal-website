---
title: 'Equivalence via surjections'
speaker: 'Tom Leinster'
---
Many categorical structures obey the following principle: the natural notion of equivalence between structures is generated, as an equivalence relation, by the equivalences that are special in two respects. First, they are genuinely surjective (not just essentially surjective) in each dimension, as well as faithful in the top dimension. Second, they preserve all kinds of composition strictly (not just up to equivalence). I will mention four instances where this principle holds. In the most complex of the four, this principle guides us to the right notion of equivalence between double categories, which is otherwise not so obvious. This is inspired by recent work of Moser, Sarazola and Verdugo.
